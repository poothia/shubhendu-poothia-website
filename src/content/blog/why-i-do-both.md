There's a strange overlap between two things I do — architecting cloud platforms and performing on stage.

In cloud architecture, you take a mess of legacy systems, vendor lock-in, and organizational inertia, and you turn it into something that works at scale. On stage, you take ordinary moments and make them land in a way nobody expects. Both are about reading context, managing complexity, and knowing when to simplify.

## Systems Thinking Applies Everywhere

When I presented at KubeCon, I realized the skills transfer directly. Timing, structure, audience awareness — these aren't peripheral. They're the difference between a talk that resonates and one that loses the room in the first five minutes.

Every production system and every performance shares the same truth: you need to have thought through the failure modes before you go live.

## Why It Matters Professionally

The best architects I know aren't just technically excellent — they communicate clearly, handle ambiguity well, and know how to bring a room of stakeholders along on a technical decision. These are skills that come from practice, and they come from unexpected places.

If you're an engineer who has hobbies that seem unrelated to tech, don't underestimate what they're teaching you.
